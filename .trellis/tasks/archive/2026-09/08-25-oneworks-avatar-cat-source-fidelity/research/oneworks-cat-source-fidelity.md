The package metadata at that revision is `@oneworks/avatar` and `@oneworks/avatar-react` `1.0.0-rc.6`; both package manifests and their per-package `LICENSE` files are MIT ([avatar package](https://github.com/oneworks-ai/avatar/blob/3ad2542ea4487e95884f313b84943df602c0e742/packages/avatar/package.json), [React package](https://github.com/oneworks-ai/avatar/blob/3ad2542ea4487e95884f313b84943df602c0e742/packages/react/package.json), [license](https://github.com/oneworks-ai/avatar/blob/3ad2542ea4487e95884f313b84943df602c0e742/LICENSE)). The tarball is evidence of cost, not a claim that registry build provenance equals the later pinned `main` revision.

**Verdict:** OneWorks' good cat read is principally a representation/occlusion result, with parameter tuning on top. Ameow's selected two-node diamond is a valid Compact result and passed its own 1x/shell gate, but it cannot reproduce the OneWorks seam solely by more width/height/position tuning: its core has binary whole-node back/front layering and no face mask for partial ear occlusion. A source-fidelity spike should test Route A first; a single adapter hurdle is not grounds to reject it.

## 1. OneWorks cat mechanics

### Model, pose, projection, and depth

- The cat is exactly three parts: two non-face `shape: 'cone'` ears and one face `shape: 'ellipse'` head ([`src/avatarEntityPresets.ts` lines 500–504](https://github.com/oneworks-ai/avatar/blob/3ad2542ea4487e95884f313b84943df602c0e742/src/avatarEntityPresets.ts#L500-L504)). Ear parameters are structural attachment inputs: left `x/y/z = -56/-78/-8`, right `56/-78/-10`, mirrored local X/Y/Z rotations, roundness 48/52, and scale about `.24 × .29`/`.23 × .28`. The face-bearing head is `.73 × .68` ellipse at `0/12/0`.
- Every part is transformed in the same pipeline. `buildAvatarBodyGeometry` applies local rotation and scale, then the shared pose yaw/pitch ([`src/avatarGeometry.ts` lines 940–976](https://github.com/oneworks-ai/avatar/blob/3ad2542ea4487e95884f313b84943df602c0e742/src/avatarGeometry.ts#L940-L976)); the part assembly projects each local center with that same pose and sorts by rotated camera depth ([`src/InteractiveAvatar.tsx` lines 296–306](https://github.com/oneworks-ai/avatar/blob/3ad2542ea4487e95884f313b84943df602c0e742/src/InteractiveAvatar.tsx#L296-L306)). This is why ears do not slide when the head turns.
- The renderer is a software 3D-to-SVG pipeline, not a static screen-space SVG. Surfaces are sampled into an outline (72 longitude × 28 latitude outline samples at the default density), visible cells are retained by normal-facing test, and cells are sorted by average transformed `z` ([`src/avatarGeometry.ts` lines 977–1033, 1062–1066](https://github.com/oneworks-ai/avatar/blob/3ad2542ea4487e95884f313b84943df602c0e742/src/avatarGeometry.ts#L977-L1066)). The surface is projected orthographically by `x/y` after pose transform ([lines 288–333](https://github.com/oneworks-ai/avatar/blob/3ad2542ea4487e95884f313b84943df602c0e742/src/avatarGeometry.ts#L288-L333)).

### Cone/rounded geometry and occlusion

- `shape: 'cone'` is procedural. For cone/frustum/half-cone profiles the radius progresses from the apex to the base; `roundness` changes the progress exponent, and the cone outline uses `roundedVertexPolygonPath` around the apex ([`src/avatarGeometry.ts` lines 378–398, 1032–1040](https://github.com/oneworks-ai/avatar/blob/3ad2542ea4487e95884f313b84943df602c0e742/src/avatarGeometry.ts#L378-L398)). Thus roundness is not merely a CSS corner radius: it changes the sampled 3D profile and the projected apex treatment. The cat preset does not use `occlusionAmount`/`occlusionPole`; those are optional cap controls for other parts.
- A face part is selected as the part with `face: true`. A non-face part with `occludedByFace: true` is masked when its transformed center is within `ENTITY_OCCLUSION_DEPTH_TOLERANCE = 24` of the face depth ([`src/InteractiveAvatar.tsx` lines 307–322](https://github.com/oneworks-ai/avatar/blob/3ad2542ea4487e95884f313b84943df602c0e742/src/InteractiveAvatar.tsx#L307-L322)). The mask paints the projected face outline black, optionally clipped to the part's occlusion cap, then applies it to that part ([lines 354–385, 393–405](https://github.com/oneworks-ai/avatar/blob/3ad2542ea4487e95884f313b84943df602c0e742/src/InteractiveAvatar.tsx#L354-L405)). The face itself is projected onto the head surface and disappears naturally at tangent views ([`src/avatarGeometry.ts` lines 1077–1125](https://github.com/oneworks-ai/avatar/blob/3ad2542ea4487e95884f313b84943df602c0e742/src/avatarGeometry.ts#L1077-L1125)).
- The silhouette blend therefore has four structural causes: (1) ears are local 3D attachments, (2) the cone has continuous rounded procedural shoulders/profile, (3) all parts share pose and are depth-ordered, and (4) the head masks ear roots while the ear tips remain visible. Parameter tuning controls the final proportion, root embedding, local tilt, scale, roundness, and depth; it cannot create any of those missing mechanisms in a flat or binary renderer.

## 2. Ameow comparison

- Production definition keeps the Kirby head as a sphere `240 × 240 × 240` and uses two `diamond` nodes `76 × 135 × 78`, positions `[-70,-76,-82]` / `[70,-76,-82]`, mirrored rotations `[0,-5,-10]` / `[0,5,10]` ([`src/presentation/main-window/compactMascotDefinition.ts` lines 31–62](../../../../src/presentation/main-window/compactMascotDefinition.ts)). The selected deep-rooted values passed the archived neutral cat-read and retained-action shell gates; candidate 4's rounded-cone parameters were rejected visually because its apex remained too sharp (calibration log table).
- Ameow still uses one shared `avatar-core` pose for both head and nodes, but `renderCompactMascotScene` renders the head and nodes through two calls and forwards only the resulting back/front path arrays ([`src/presentation/main-window/compactMascotBehaviorRuntime.ts` lines 31–52](../../../../src/presentation/main-window/compactMascotBehaviorRuntime.ts)). `CompactMascot` has four fixed ear slots, one head path, and a head clip for eyes; it is explicitly not a general body-node renderer ([`CompactMascot.tsx` lines 58–80, 139–180](../../../../src/presentation/main-window/CompactMascot.tsx)).
- In the authoritative `@bible-strong/avatar-core` geometry, each accessory is sampled and projected in the shared pose, but `accessoryLayers` computes one center depth plus a projected depth radius and classifies the entire node as `front` or `back`; it sorts those whole paths and returns two arrays ([`packages/avatar-core/src/geometry.ts` lines 1155–1234](../../../../.cindy-upstream/bible-strong-avatar-lab-175691a/packages/avatar-core/src/geometry.ts)). `renderAvatar` then emits back paths, head, and front paths ([same file lines 1237–1260](../../../../.cindy-upstream/bible-strong-avatar-lab-175691a/packages/avatar-core/src/geometry.ts)). There is no OneWorks-style per-part face mask or per-fragment ear/head occlusion in this Compact seam.

**Representation verdict:** parameter-level tuning is sufficient for the approved Ameow look (and should remain frozen), but is insufficient for OneWorks source fidelity. OneWorks' rounded procedural cone plus head-derived mask is a representation/occlusion change, not another ear-size candidate. The current lifecycle, Pointer Field, Reduced Motion, 60px shell, and presentation authority are architecture constraints, not reasons to alter the archived mascot in this research task.

## 3. Routes A/B/C

| Route | Feasibility and fidelity | Main risks / boundary obligations | Recommendation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A — direct `@oneworks/avatar-react` / upstream renderer** | Highest source fidelity and lowest geometry reimplementation. `Avatar` accepts a definition and animation libraries and renders the same InteractiveAvatar pipeline ([React entry lines 64–76, 280–348](https://github.com/oneworks-ai/avatar/blob/3ad2542ea4487e95884f313b84943df602c0e742/packages/react/src/index.tsx#L64-L76)). React 19.1 in Ameow satisfies peer `>=18.3 <20`; Electron's Chromium supplies the DOM. | Published runtime is a meaningful bundle addition (318.6KB JS plus 65.5KB CSS before app bundling); React package depends on `@oneworks/avatar`. `Avatar` has drag interaction, not Compact Pointer Field follow; it has no Reduced Motion prop, so an adapter must own pointer-to-`scene.view` mapping and suppress/reset playback in Reduced Motion. Its aspect-ratio host has no 60px Compact semantics, so shell sizing and lifecycle remain Ameow-owned. MIT notices must be retained. | **Do first as a Lab-only spike**, then consider production only if bundle/import and lifecycle evidence pass. Keep Ameow's Compact authority above the renderer; do not let `onDefinitionChange` or upstream animation state own Product/native lifecycle. |
| **B — port OneWorks geometry/projection** | Good fidelity if the port preserves local transforms, rounded cone sampling, shared pose, depth ordering, and head masks. It can remain under Ameow's existing runtime and Pointer Field, and avoid shipping the full editor. | Cross-implementation maintenance and numerical/SVG differences; porting just cone vertices without occlusion is not equivalent. MIT permits port with attribution. Needs explicit tests for yaw/pitch, tangent visibility, mask seams, shell bounds, Reduced Motion, and Electron. | **Second**, only after A's visual and integration evidence identifies exactly what must be ported. |
| **C — retain renderer + adopt semantics** | Smallest package/runtime change. Current renderer already has local nodes, shared pose, sampling, and depth-aware back/front classification. | If “retain renderer” excludes representation-level changes, it cannot reproduce partial head occlusion or OneWorks rounded-cone shoulders; further numeric tuning repeats the failed cone/diamond tradeoff. If it includes new cone profile + masks, it converges on B while retaining more Ameow code. | **Last / conditional**: use only if A is visually accepted but operationally too costly, and after isolating a minimal semantic subset. |

All routes can comply with MIT; license is not the deciding blocker. A direct production renderer must remain subordinate to Compact presentation authority: `CompactMascot` owns mount/dispose, native-window visibility, Pointer Field reads, Reduced Motion, and the unchanged 60px shell. A source-fidelity spike must prove these at a side-by-side Lab mount before any production decision.

## 5. Production/tooling isolation

`AvatarEditor` is a full authoring application, not a production Compact primitive. The spike should import and mount it only from a UI Lab/dev-only entry, with `@oneworks/avatar-react/style.css` scoped or loaded by that Lab entry. The production Compact import graph must not import `AvatarEditor`, `App`, `AvatarControls`, `AnimationPanel`, or editor CSS. Editor definition changes may be captured as research artifacts or explicit fixtures, but must never flow into Compact lifecycle/native/Product state. A production `Avatar` renderer experiment, if needed, should likewise be a separate opt-in Lab surface until the bundle, lifecycle, pointer, and Reduced Motion gates pass.

## 6. Minimal source-fidelity integration spike

**Hypothesis:** at pinned revision `3ad2542…`, the public `@oneworks/avatar-react` `Avatar` can render the upstream cat at the real 60px Compact shell and at a magnified Lab size with a thin pointer/reduced-motion adapter, while preserving Ameow's existing presentation authority. If it fails visually, the evidence will identify whether cone profile, pose/depth, or head masking is the missing mechanism before any port.

**Scope:** Lab-only, no archived mascot edits. Mount `Avatar` from the exact pinned source/package in a side-by-side harness; feed a fixed cat definition, expose a 60px square and a large inspection view, sweep yaw/pitch through front/side/back and a pointer-follow trace, and run normal/reduced-motion modes. Separately mount `AvatarEditor` in the same Lab harness to verify the public API and the capability table above. Keep definition/playback local to the harness; do not connect Compact lifecycle/native/Product state.

**Evidence outputs:** tight crops at 60px and magnified scale for neutral, ±yaw/±pitch, root occlusion, tangent eye visibility, pointer approach, Reduced Motion, and mount/dispose; an API/state log proving controlled `definition`/`onDefinitionChange`; a bundle/import report proving editor code is Lab-only; and a short route decision record. Use the existing shell bound and pointer traces as comparison inputs, not new production tuning.

**Pass:** (1) cat silhouette and ear-root blend are visibly superior or equivalent at 60px and magnified size; (2) one shared pose keeps head, ears, and face attached through the sweep; (3) head occlusion changes naturally without detached wedges or accidental shell clipping; (4) pointer follow and Reduced Motion remain owned by the adapter/Compact boundary; (5) mount, unmount, hidden/visible, and animation stop/dispose leave no pending renderer work; and (6) production build/import evidence excludes editor tooling.

**Fail / stop:** stop the spike and report the failing mechanism if the pinned public renderer cannot be mounted in the Electron/React runtime, if controlled pose/pointer and Reduced Motion cannot be kept outside upstream ownership, if the 60px shell cannot be preserved without production-specific hacks, or if the production import graph pulls the full editor. A single CSS adaptation issue, missing convenience prop, or one bad preset is not a rejection; fix it in the thin Lab adapter or collect the next bounded evidence. Only after a mechanism-level failure should the team proceed to Route B, then Route C.
